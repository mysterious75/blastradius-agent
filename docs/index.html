Three questions, asked before deployment

Most tooling in this space is retrospective — it tells you what already happened. These are the questions that only make sense before a listing goes live.

01 · Reachability

How far does the damage travel?

Token → markets that accept it → protocols hosting them → every chain they touch. Mapped transitively, with the TVL at each hop.

02 · Solvency

What survives liquidation?

When collateral goes to zero the debt borrowed against it does not. Liquidators cannot liquidate a position with nothing to seize. We project that gap per market.

03 · Redundancy

Can one compromised signer sign it all away?

Cross-chain verification stacks, multisig thresholds, oracle redundancy, backstops. Under-redundant configuration is flagged before it ships.

Case study: one signer, $292M

On 18 April 2026 a cross-chain messaging pathway released 116,500 rsETH to an attacker. No smart contract was exploited. Every contract behaved exactly as designed. The failure was a configuration: the receive pathway required a single verifier.

pathway config requiredDVNCount = 1, optionalDVNCount = 0 | v 1 forged cross-chain packet source transaction never existed | v 116,500 rsETH released (~$292M) -- no contract bug involved | +---------------------------+---------------------------+ v v v posted as collateral borrowed against it second packet (40k) on a lending market -> nothing to seize blocked 46 min later | | by the pause role v v market frozen unliquidatable bad debt across protocols absorbed by the pool's own reserves | v wrapped copies on 20+ chains questioned

Facts summarised from the public incident write-up and on-chain record. Source: Blockaid's post-mortem (19 Apr 2026). Figures are that incident's; nothing from that write-up is reproduced beyond the factual outline above.

BlastRadius reports both halves of that failure: the configuration (requiredDVNCount < 2) and the contagion (which markets and chains inherit the loss). Run the shipped config snapshot through the auditor to see it catch the exact misconfiguration.

About

BlastRadius is an open-source, research-stage toolkit for pre-deployment contagion risk in decentralised finance. It reads published configuration and market data and reports risk estimates.

Not financial, legal or investment advice. Figures are point-in-time estimates and stale the moment they are written.

Data

Market figures are from DeFiLlama's free public APIs, attributed and unmodified except for a documented join.

Derived figures (bad debt, uncovered loss, blast radius scores) are computed locally and are not DeFiLlama figures. See DATA_ATTRIBUTION.md.

Independence

No affiliation with, and no endorsement by, any protocol, company or incident party named here. All names are used for identification only. No third-party logos or marks are used.

Incident figures belong to their respective parties and are summarised with source attribution.

Configurations are reported so that they can be fixed before deployment. This toolkit produces risk findings only — it generates no payloads, runs no traffic against third-party systems, and is intended solely for assets and systems you own or are authorised to assess.
